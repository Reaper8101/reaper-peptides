AR // Peptide Basics

New to Peptides? The Basics.

01 // THE BASICS

What Are Peptides?

Peptides are small building blocks made from amino acids. Some help carry messages in the body. Different peptides do different things.

02 // DIFFERENT GOALS

Why Are There So Many?

Researchers study different compounds for things like appetite, sleep, skin, recovery, and muscle growth.

03 // DIFFERENT SCHEDULES

Daily or Weekly?

It depends on the compound. Some are studied daily, others weekly, and some for shorter periods. There is no one-size-fits-all schedule.

04 // COMBINING COMPOUNDS

Can They Be Combined?

Some compounds are studied together. But different goals do not always mean they are safe to mix. Their effects can overlap, so each combination needs to be checked.

Tirzepatide

  • Appetite control
  • Weight management
  • Blood sugar regulation
  • Longer-lasting fullness
Key Benefits & Research Interests

Appetite Control: Helps quiet hunger signals and reduce food cravings.

Weight Management: Supports meaningful weight reduction through reduced food intake.

Blood Sugar: Improves glucose-dependent insulin response and blood-sugar regulation.

Fullness: Helps meals feel satisfying for longer.

Retatrutide

  • Weight management
  • Appetite control
  • Energy expenditure
  • Glucose regulation
Key Benefits & Research Interests

Weight Management: Has produced substantial weight-loss results in clinical research.

Appetite Control: Helps lower hunger and support smaller portions.

Energy Expenditure: Its glucagon action can increase energy use.

Metabolic Health: Influences glucose regulation and how the body uses stored fuel.

5-Amino-1MQ

  • Fat metabolism research
  • Cellular energy balance
  • Metabolic pathways
  • Body composition research
Key Benefits & Research Interests

NNMT Inhibition: Blocks an enzyme involved in metabolic regulation.

Fat-Tissue Biology: Studied for effects on how fat cells manage and store energy.

Energy Balance: Studied for its effects on metabolic signaling.

Body Composition: Studied for fat metabolism and weight-related pathways.

Eloralintide

  • Appetite regulation
  • Fullness signaling
  • Weight management
  • Amylin pathways
Key Benefits & Research Interests

Appetite Regulation: Activates pathways that help control hunger.

Feeling Full: Supports satiety signals that make smaller meals more satisfying.

Weight Management: Being evaluated for its ability to reduce food intake and body weight.

Metabolic Health: Studied alongside weight-related measures such as glucose regulation.

Lipo-C Fat Blaster

  • L-carnitine and fat transport
  • B vitamins for energy metabolism
  • Choline and inositol pathways
  • Methionine and nutrient processing
Research Overview

Fat transport: L-carnitine helps move certain fats into mitochondria, where they can be used for energy.

Energy support: B6 and B12 help the body process nutrients and support normal energy metabolism.

Other ingredients: Methionine, inositol, and choline take part in normal fat handling and liver-related nutrient pathways.

Formula overview: Contains L-carnitine 300 mg/mL, methionine 25 mg/mL, inositol 50 mg/mL, choline 50 mg/mL, B12 1 mg/mL, B6 50 mg/mL, and NADH 50 mg/mL. The blend itself has not been shown to cause fat loss.

SLU-PP-332 (SLUPP)

  • Exercise-like effects in animal studies
  • Increased endurance in mice
  • Fat-burning and energy-use pathways
  • More energy-producing machinery in muscle
Research Overview

Exercise-related pathways: SLU-PP-332 activates proteins called estrogen-related receptors (ERRs). These help regulate how muscle cells make and use energy.

Endurance research: In published mouse studies, SLU-PP-332 increased running endurance and shifted muscle toward greater aerobic activity.

Fat and energy use: Animal research found changes in fat-burning pathways and energy use, making it interesting for metabolism and weight-management research.

Cellular energy: Laboratory and mouse studies reported increases in mitochondria, the structures that help cells produce energy. These findings have not established benefits in humans.

SS-31

  • Mitochondrial protection
  • Cell energy support
  • Cellular resilience
  • Tissue function
Key Benefits & Research Interests

Mitochondrial Support: Interacts with the inner mitochondrial membrane to help preserve its structure.

Cell Energy: Supports the machinery cells use to produce usable energy.

Cell Protection: Studied for reducing mitochondrial stress in demanding conditions.

Tissue Function: Attracts interest in energy-hungry tissues such as muscle and heart.

NAD+

  • Cellular energy
  • DNA maintenance
  • Metabolic processes
  • Healthy aging pathways
Key Benefits & Research Interests

Cell Fuel: Carries electrons that help cells convert food into usable energy.

Energy Production: Plays a central role in mitochondrial energy metabolism.

DNA Repair: Is used by enzymes involved in responding to DNA damage.

Cell Maintenance: Supports normal metabolic signaling and cellular upkeep.

MOTS-C

  • Mitochondrial signaling
  • Metabolic flexibility
  • Glucose use
  • Exercise adaptation
Key Benefits & Research Interests

Metabolic Flexibility: Studied for how cells adapt when energy demands change.

Fuel Utilization: Influences pathways involved in using and managing available energy.

Glucose Handling: Explored for its relationship with insulin sensitivity.

Exercise Response: Draws interest for signaling associated with physical and metabolic stress.

Glutathione

  • Major antioxidant in the body
  • Protects cells from oxidative stress
  • Supports normal liver detox pathways
  • Helps recycle other antioxidants
Research Overview

Cell protection: Glutathione is one of the body’s main antioxidants. It helps protect cells from damage caused by normal metabolism and environmental stress.

Liver function: The liver uses glutathione in natural processes that help handle and remove certain unwanted substances.

Antioxidant recycling: Glutathione helps maintain the balance of other antioxidants, including vitamins C and E.

Research interest: Scientists study glutathione in oxidative stress, cellular health, and recovery. Effects depend on how it is used and have not been established for every advertised purpose.

Memory Pathways: Attracts interest in learning and cognitive signaling.

DSIP

  • Sleep regulation
  • Restful sleep research
  • Stress response
  • Recovery pathways
Key Benefits & Research Interests

Sleep Regulation: Investigated for interactions with biological sleep pathways.

Rest & Relaxation: Studied for sleep-related nervous-system signaling.

Stress Response: Studied for links between stress physiology and sleep.

Recovery: Sleep and recovery are closely connected, making this a common research focus.

Selank + Semax

  • Focus and attention research
  • Memory and learning pathways
  • Stress-response regulation
  • Calmness and mental clarity interest
Research Overview

Semax and focus: Semax is studied for attention, memory, and brain-signaling pathways, including systems involved in learning.

Selank and stress: Selank is studied for stress responses and anxiety-related signaling, with interest in a calmer mental state.

Why combine them: The blend brings together two different research interests: cognitive performance and stress regulation.

Research limits: Most evidence is limited, and a combined Selank + Semax product has not been shown to deliver all of these outcomes in controlled human trials.

  • Body composition research

Dual-Pathway Research: Combines CJC-1295 without DAC and Ipamorelin, which act through different receptor pathways involved in growth hormone release.

GH & IGF-1 Signaling: Research explores how these signals interact with the growth hormone and IGF-1 systems.

Body Composition: Investigated in connection with hormone pathways related to lean tissue and fat metabolism.

Recovery Biology: Studied for growth hormone pulses and tissue maintenance signaling.

IGF-1 LR3

  • Growth signaling
  • Protein synthesis pathways
  • Muscle research
  • Tissue development
Key Benefits & Research Interests

Cell Growth: Activates receptors involved in growth-related signaling.

Protein Metabolism: Connects with pathways that regulate tissue building and maintenance.

Nutrient Signaling: Influences cellular responses to nutrients and glucose.

Muscle Research: Of interest in studies of muscle-cell growth and repair biology.

Super Human Blend

  • Nitric-oxide and blood-flow pathways
  • Amino acids involved in recovery
  • Muscle energy and nutrient transport
  • Antioxidant support from NAC
Research Overview

Circulation: Arginine and citrulline are involved in nitric-oxide pathways, which help blood vessels relax and support circulation.

Recovery building blocks: Lysine, glutamine, proline, and ornithine are amino acids involved in normal tissue maintenance and metabolism.

Energy and protection: Carnitine is involved in fat transport, taurine supports several cell functions, and NAC supplies building blocks used to make glutathione.

Blend details: Per 10 mL: arginine 110 mg, ornithine 110 mg, citrulline 120 mg, lysine 70 mg, glutamine 40 mg, proline 60 mg, taurine 60 mg, carnitine 220 mg, NAC 75 mg. These roles do not establish a proven performance benefit from this mixture.

FLGR-242

  • Proposed myostatin inhibition
  • Potential muscle-growth signaling
  • Research into limits on muscle size
  • Unverified human effects
Research Overview

Myostatin inhibition: FLGR-242 is promoted as a possible myostatin inhibitor. Myostatin is a natural protein that helps limit how much muscle grows. Blocking this signal is a major area of muscle-growth research.

Why it interests researchers: Reducing myostatin activity could, in theory, allow greater muscle growth and influence lean body mass. This is the proposed benefit, not a proven effect of FLGR-242.

Muscle growth versus strength: More muscle tissue does not automatically mean stronger tendons or better performance. Scientists study these outcomes separately.

What is actually known: The identity, target activity, and human results for products sold as FLGR-242 are not reliably documented. Its claimed myostatin-inhibiting action should be treated as unverified.

SNAP-8

  • Softening expression-line appearance
  • Cosmetic wrinkle research
  • Facial muscle-signal pathways
  • Smoother-looking skin interest
Research Overview

Expression lines: SNAP-8, also called acetyl octapeptide-3, is a cosmetic peptide studied for reducing the appearance of lines caused by repeated facial expressions.

How it is proposed to work: It is designed to interfere with part of the signaling process involved in facial muscle movement, which may help expression lines look less noticeable.

Skin appearance: Its main research interest is smoother-looking skin, especially around common expression-line areas.

What to expect from evidence: Evidence for visible results is limited and largely cosmetic. It is not a proven substitute for medical wrinkle treatments and is not a collagen-building peptide.
