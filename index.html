AR // Peptide Basics

New to Peptides? Start Here.

Popular names like Ozempic® and Mounjaro® are peptide-based medications you may already recognize. Peptides are small chains of amino acids that researchers study because they can act as signals in the body. Different compounds are researched for different areas, including weight management, recovery, sleep, cellular energy, healthy aging and body composition.

01 // The Basics

What Are Peptides?

Amino acids are often called the building blocks of proteins. Peptides are simply shorter chains of those amino acids. Researchers study them because different peptides can interact with different processes in the body.

02 // Different Research Areas

Why Are There So Many?

Each compound has a different research focus. Some are studied mainly for appetite and metabolism, while others are researched for recovery, energy, sleep, healthy aging, brain health or muscle-related pathways.

03 // The Powder

Why Is It Freeze-Dried?

Many research peptides come as a dry powder instead of a liquid. This is called freeze-drying, or lyophilization. It is commonly used to help keep research material stable during storage and transportation.

04 // Keep It Simple

Don't Know the Science? That's Okay.

Names like MOTS-C, SS-31 and CJC-1295 can look complicated. You do not need to understand all of the chemistry to browse the research. Each compound card below gives you the simple version first, with more detail available if you want it.

Tirzepatide

Tirzepatide targets two appetite and blood sugar pathways, helping people eat less, stay satisfied, and lose weight. Its weight-loss and glucose benefits are backed by large clinical trials.

Less Hunger • Weight Loss • Steadier Blood Sugar • Lasting Fullness
Main Effects

Less Hunger: Reduces appetite and food cravings, making smaller portions easier to stick with.

Weight Loss: Supports substantial weight reduction by lowering calorie intake over time.

Steadier Blood Sugar: Improves insulin release when glucose rises and helps control blood sugar after meals.

Lasting Fullness: Slows stomach emptying and strengthens fullness signals so meals satisfy longer.

Retatrutide

Retatrutide activates three pathways at once: GLP-1, GIP, and glucagon. Together, they reduce hunger, improve glucose regulation, and increase energy expenditure. Clinical trials have shown substantial weight loss.

Appetite Control • Stored-Fuel Use • Blood Sugar Balance • Weight Reduction
Main Effects

Appetite Control: Turns down hunger and supports smaller meals without constant snacking.

Stored-Fuel Use: Its glucagon action raises energy expenditure and changes how the body handles stored fuel, including fat.

Blood Sugar Balance: GLP-1 and GIP actions improve glucose-dependent insulin signaling and blood sugar regulation.

Weight Reduction: Combining lower food intake with changes in energy use has delivered major weight-loss results in clinical trials.

KLOW

KLOW combines GHK-Cu, BPC-157, TB-500, and KPV. The blend brings together compounds investigated for skin repair, soft-tissue healing, and inflammation control; reliable human results for the blend have not been established.

Skin & Collagen • Soft-Tissue Repair • Inflammation Signals • Recovery Blend
Main Effects

Skin & Collagen: GHK-Cu influences collagen-related processes and is of interest for skin structure and repair.

Soft-Tissue Repair: BPC-157 and TB-500 are being explored for how tissue responds to injury and strain, mostly in preclinical studies.

Inflammation Signals: KPV affects pathways involved in inflammatory responses in early research.

Recovery Blend: Four different ingredients target different repair pathways. The combined effect in people remains unproven.

Epitalon

Epitalon is a short peptide associated with cellular aging and sleep-cycle research. It attracts attention for its effects on cell processes, but claims of longer life or better sleep in humans remain unproven.

Cell Aging • Telomeres • Sleep Timing • Long-Term Health
Main Effects

Cell Aging: Scientists examine how Epitalon interacts with processes connected to cellular aging.

Telomeres: Early experiments have explored telomere-related activity, a key topic in cellular longevity.

Sleep Timing: Its possible connection to daily sleep-wake rhythms is a research interest, not a proven sleep treatment.

Long-Term Health: Longevity claims are not established by strong human trials, so its real-world benefit is still uncertain.

SS-31

SS-31, also called elamipretide, acts inside mitochondria, the structures that power cells. It is designed to stabilize their inner membranes and support more efficient cell function.

Mitochondrial Support • Cell Stress • Muscle Function • Heart & Tissue Health
Main Effects

Mitochondrial Support: Interacts with the inner mitochondrial membrane, helping preserve the structure cells need to make energy.

Cell Stress: Can reduce signs of mitochondrial stress in laboratory and animal studies.

Muscle Function: Muscle cells depend heavily on mitochondria, making endurance and muscle function important research targets.

Heart & Tissue Health: Also investigated in tissues with high energy demands, including the heart; outcomes depend on the condition studied.

NAD+

NAD+ is essential for turning food into usable cellular energy. It also powers enzymes involved in DNA repair and normal cell maintenance. These are established roles of the molecule, not proof that NAD+ injections improve performance or slow aging.

Cell Energy • DNA Repair • Metabolic Function • Healthy Cell Maintenance
Main Effects

Cell Energy: Helps cells convert nutrients into the energy they need to function.

DNA Repair: Supplies key enzymes that help cells respond to DNA damage.

Metabolic Function: Plays a central role in how the body handles and uses fuel.

Healthy Cell Maintenance: Supports normal cellular processes; added NAD+ has not been proven to reverse aging.

MOTS-C

MOTS-C is a small peptide linked to how cells respond to metabolic stress. Animal studies have shown effects on fuel use and insulin sensitivity; human performance and fat-loss benefits are not yet established.

Fuel Management • Glucose Handling • Exercise Response • Metabolic Health
Main Effects

Fuel Management: Affects pathways involved in how cells respond to changes in available energy.

Glucose Handling: Early studies have examined improvements in insulin sensitivity and glucose use.

Exercise Response: Its relationship to physical stress and adaptation is an active area of research.

Metabolic Health: Promising preclinical findings have not yet translated into proven weight-loss or endurance results in people.

CJC-1295 Without DAC

CJC-1295 without DAC stimulates the signaling pathway that releases growth hormone. It acts upstream of GH, rather than supplying growth hormone directly.

GH Release • IGF-1 Signaling • Tissue Maintenance • Recovery Questions
Main Effects

GH Release: Activates receptors that tell the pituitary gland to release growth hormone.

IGF-1 Signaling: Growth hormone can increase IGF-1, a signal involved in tissue growth and maintenance.

Tissue Maintenance: The GH and IGF-1 system helps regulate muscle, bone, and other tissues.

Recovery Questions: Better sleep, faster recovery, and improved body composition are not established clinical outcomes for this compound.

Ipamorelin

Ipamorelin stimulates the ghrelin receptor to trigger growth-hormone release. It is a targeted GH secretagogue, but real-world effects on sleep, muscle gain, and fat loss have not been established in robust clinical trials.

GH Release • Growth Pathways • Body Composition • Recovery & Sleep
Main Effects

GH Release: Signals the pituitary gland to release growth hormone.

Growth Pathways: Growth hormone contributes to tissue maintenance and regulation of IGF-1.

Body Composition: Its influence on GH makes body composition a research focus, not a guaranteed result.

Recovery & Sleep: Claims of deeper sleep and faster recovery need stronger human evidence.

Pinealon

Pinealon is a short experimental peptide explored for brain-cell protection. Most evidence comes from laboratory and animal work, not clinical studies proving better memory or focus.

Brain Cells • Oxidative Stress • Memory & Learning • Brain Aging
Main Effects

Brain Cells: Investigated for effects on cell function and survival under stress.

Oxidative Stress: Early research examines how it influences damage caused by reactive molecules.

Memory & Learning: Cognitive outcomes are a research question; improved memory in people is not established.

Brain Aging: Long-term protection against cognitive decline has not been demonstrated in human trials.

5-Amino-1MQ

5-Amino-1MQ blocks NNMT, an enzyme involved in cellular metabolism. Early experiments connect NNMT inhibition with changes in fat tissue and energy balance, but human fat-loss benefits are unproven.

NNMT Blockade • Fat-Tissue Biology • Energy Balance • Body Composition
Main Effects

NNMT Blockade: Directly inhibits NNMT, changing an enzyme pathway linked to energy handling.

Fat-Tissue Biology: Animal studies have explored its effects on fat tissue and weight-related processes.

Energy Balance: Changes in metabolic signaling are a key reason the compound attracts research interest.

Body Composition: Reliable human evidence for faster fat loss or muscle preservation is still lacking.

DSIP

DSIP stands for delta sleep-inducing peptide. It has a long history in sleep and stress research, but evidence that it consistently improves sleep in people remains limited.

Sleep Signals • Stress Response • Sleep Quality • Recovery
Main Effects

Sleep Signals: Associated with sleep-related biological pathways in experimental research.

Stress Response: Researchers have examined its relationship to stress and nervous-system activity.

Sleep Quality: Claims of deeper or longer sleep are not consistently supported by human trials.

Recovery: Better rest can support recovery, but DSIP itself has not been proven to speed it up.

Somatotropin

Somatotropin is human growth hormone (HGH). It regulates growth, tissue maintenance, and the body’s use of fat and other fuels. Its medically established benefits depend on the condition being treated.

Growth & Repair • Protein Building • Fat Metabolism • Bone Health
Main Effects

Growth & Repair: Stimulates growth and supports maintenance of muscle, bone, and other tissues.

Protein Building: Promotes protein synthesis and influences lean-body-mass regulation.

Fat Metabolism: Increases fat breakdown and affects how the body selects fuel.

Bone Health: Supports normal bone growth and turnover; taking extra HGH is not a shortcut to stronger bones or better athletic performance.

IGF-1 LR3

IGF-1 LR3 is a modified form of IGF-1 designed for longer biological activity. IGF signaling helps regulate cell growth and nutrient use, but claims of extra muscle growth and faster recovery from IGF-1 LR3 are not established in people.

Cell Growth • Protein Metabolism • Nutrient Signaling • Muscle & Recovery
Main Effects

Cell Growth: Activates growth-related signaling pathways in cells.

Protein Metabolism: IGF pathways play a role in building and maintaining tissues.

Nutrient Signaling: Influences how cells respond to nutrients, including glucose.

Muscle & Recovery: Animal and laboratory findings do not establish safe or reliable muscle gains in humans.

Kisspeptin

Kisspeptin starts the reproductive hormone chain. It triggers GnRH release in the brain, which directs LH and FSH secretion and helps control fertility and sex-hormone production.

Hormone Regulation • Testosterone Signaling • Fertility & Ovulation • Sexual Response
Main Effects

Hormone Regulation: Triggers GnRH, the signal that tells the pituitary gland to release LH and FSH.

Testosterone Signaling: LH directs the testes to make testosterone. Kisspeptin acts earlier in that hormone chain.

Fertility & Ovulation: Helps coordinate reproductive processes and has been used experimentally to trigger egg maturation in IVF settings.

Sexual Response: Human experiments show effects on brain responses to sexual and emotional cues; that is not the same as proven treatment for sexual dysfunction.

Eloralintide

Eloralintide activates amylin receptors involved in hunger and fullness. Its goal is straightforward: reduce food intake and support weight loss. Clinical research is evaluating how well those effects hold up over time.

Appetite Control • Feeling Full • Weight Loss • Metabolic Health
Main Effects

Appetite Control: Targets signals involved in hunger, helping reduce the drive to eat.

Feeling Full: Acts on satiety pathways so smaller meals can feel more satisfying.

Weight Loss: Lower food intake is the main way this compound aims to reduce body weight.

Metabolic Health: Researchers are measuring how weight changes affect markers such as blood sugar; long-term outcomes are still being determined.
